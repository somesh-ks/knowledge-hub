# AI, Design and 2 more

A: Hello, this is Singularity, and Owen is joining me today.

B: Glad to be here. We have a full run today, from why the user is leaving UX, to ethical design, a yo-yo headphone concept, and touchscreens on MacBooks.

A: Plus AI tool choices, a Guardian design system, a Motorola flagship, and a ChatGPT security flaw. Let's start with UX.

A: Nielsen Norman Group says its field is now experience design, not user experience, because AI agents act for people. It had already retitled its specialists from user-experience specialist to experience specialist. One author who doubted that change now backs it.

B: Okay, but why drop the user? That word has been the core of the field for a long time.

A: Don Norman popularized the term user experience in the 1990s, and journey maps followed in the 2010s as smartphones multiplied channels. Through every stage, one assumption held: the human was the user.

B: So in practice, the person at the screen may not be the one doing the clicking. An agent could be acting for them, and that is why the word user starts to feel too narrow.

A: That fits what Nielsen Norman Group describes. The field now covers the experience of people whose tasks software handles for them, and that is why it chose the name experience design.

A: Next, ethical design. A piece on UX Collective says it lets people see an influence and still choose otherwise. The author's example is a cookie banner, where Accept all is a big button and Reject sits smaller and a layer down.

B: So the nudge lives in the layout. Having a Reject option isn't enough if it's built to be missed. That's my reading, anyway.

A: Autoplay steers the same way, by removing the pause where a viewer might decide to stop. She defines autonomy through Self-Determination Theory, as acting with volition, not merely having options.

A: Now a design concept. All Design Lab's Jojo is a wired headphone that uses its cable as the headband. Turning an ear cup's outer cover winds cable in or lets it out like a yo-yo, and a ratchet holds the chosen length in steps.

B: So you size the headband by twisting the cup. That sounds clever, though I would want to know how it feels on a long listen.

A: The cushion is meant to stay still against the ear while the cover rotates, and an inline remote holds the microphone and volume rocker. It is still a concept, so comfort, grip during movement, winding wear and repairability are open questions.

A: On AI tools, Neeman argues that practitioners, not operations, should choose a team's tools. He cites Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index, where 78% of AI users at work brought their own tools.

B: So people are already picking tools themselves, and the argument is that the people doing the work should have the say.

A: He also points to Anthropic's preliminary study of about 400,000 Claude Code sessions. Expert sessions reached verified success more than twice as often as novice ones.

A: Apple is adding touchscreens to MacBook Pros, 16 years after Jobs panned them. In 2010 Jobs said touch surfaces don't want to be vertical, and that after extended use an arm wants to fall off.

B: That's quite a turnaround. And Apple isn't first here, right?

A: Right. Microsoft Surface laptops and many Windows on ARM and x86 laptops already have touch. The new MacBooks have no 360° hinge, so they can't fold into tablet mode like convertibles.

A: The Guardian maintains Stand, a component library and design system for its internal tools, as the npm package @guardian/stand. It holds two collections: the Tools Design System, covering colour, typography and buttons, and shareable components such as a byline editor.

B: So even if you don't use React, you can still take the design tokens. That sounds handy.

A: Stand is built with React, Emotion and TypeScript, but its CSS variables and JS tokens can be imported without them. One catch: inclusion in the package does not mean a component is part of the design system.

A: Motorola has confirmed that the Signature 27, its first proper flagship in years, launches in the US in November. A landing page on its US site has carried that month since the September announcement, though the company had publicly offered only a vague coming-months timeline.

B: So November is the month, but you can't mark a day on the calendar yet.

A: Correct, Motorola has not given an exact date. The phone uses Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 chip.

A: That's Singularity for today. Thanks, Owen, and thanks for listening.
