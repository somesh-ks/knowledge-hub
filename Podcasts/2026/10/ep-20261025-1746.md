# Nati's week

Hello, and welcome to Singularity, your private briefing on tech and design. This week: a way into Atomic Design that starts from what a screen's actions change, then a run of shorter items on design tokens, CSS masonry, generative interfaces, a foldable phone, and elections abroad.

An essay on UX Collective proposes entering Atomic Design from the screen. The question to ask is what each user action changes. A sort button sits in one column header, but it reorders the whole table. So position is a poor guide to what connects. The author puts shared rules in the template level. One example is a filter change resetting pagination. The catch is that this scope breakdown is not universal. Scopes differ between screens and products.

A Modeinspect guide traces one design token through four hops from Figma to a Tailwind class. The hops are a JSON export, CSS variables, a theme mapping, and the class itself. The theme owns the names and values. A Figma export seeds it once, and later exports arrive as reviewed diffs. Going back from code to Figma stays manual for most teams. Automating the sync through Figma's variables API needs an Enterprise Full seat.

CSS now has native masonry, the Pinterest-style layout where items pack into columns of uneven height. It goes by the name display: grid-lanes. The CSS Working Group agreed in January 2025 to reuse Grid's track properties, and it settled on the name by December 2025. You define columns with grid-template-columns, and the browser places each item, usually in the shortest lane. No item heights are needed. The spec is still an Editor's Draft, so details can change.

Google Research published a Generative UI system in November 2025, built on Gemini 3 Pro. It creates interactive pages and tools for each prompt, and it is rolling out in the Gemini app and in AI Mode in Search. In Google's tests, raters preferred it to plain text answers. Sites designed by human experts still ranked higher. Generation can take a minute or more, with occasional inaccuracies.

The iPhone Duo, Apple's first foldable phone, sold out immediately and is backordered until next year. Chance Miller of 9to5Mac spent a week with it ahead of launch. His hands-on review covers the phone's poses, the state of the software, Touch ID versus Face ID, and battery life. Apple has called pre-order demand extraordinary, and shipping dates have slipped. Apple has also announced a product event for October 28, called Pro and tell.

Israel is pressing ahead with a West Bank operation. It is focused on the city of Hebron, the site of Friday's attack. The operation comes as Israel enters the final days before a tense election. The vote is largely seen as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's leadership.

Venezuelan opposition leader María Machado has returned to the country after months in exile. She had left just a month before the US government seized Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro.

Brazil is holding a presidential runoff today that will decide who leads the country. The race is on a knife edge. It is between incumbent left-wing President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and right-wing Senator Flávio Bolsonaro.

One more on Brazil. The runoff pits the left-of-center veteran politician Lula against Bolsonaro, an ally of US President Donald Trump. Bolsonaro is the son of imprisoned former President Jair Bolsonaro. U.S. influence over Latin America is seen to be at stake.

That's the briefing. Until next time.
