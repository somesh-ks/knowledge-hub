# World, Business and 2 more

This is Singularity. Today: diplomatic tensions in Korea and Jerusalem, a major Hollywood merger, new AI moves from Google and the White House, and the Nobel Prize in physics.

South Korea has recalled its ambassador to Ukraine over a dispute about North Korean prisoners of war. Ukraine's president revealed that two North Korean detainees were transferred to Seoul, a move South Korea says was supposed to be confidential. The recall signals a sharp diplomatic protest and raises questions about how allies share sensitive information. With tensions already high on the Korean peninsula, this incident could complicate coordination on regional security. The situation remains tense as both countries assess next steps.

The United States has filed a new indictment against former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, accusing him of conspiring to torture Venezuelan and American citizens. This adds to existing charges related to drugs and weapons. The allegations paint a broader picture of human rights abuses under his leadership. While Maduro remains in Venezuela, the charges deepen his legal isolation. The case may influence how other nations view his government.

The UK says its diplomatic presence in East Jerusalem will continue, but under a new name. The consulate building will now be called the 'UK Mission, Jerusalem'. Foreign Secretary Miliband confirmed the office remains open, though the change in title may signal a shift in policy. The move comes amid ongoing debate over diplomatic recognition in the region. Observers are watching whether this affects the UK's stance on Palestinian statehood.

South Korea has successfully test-flown its first domestically developed hypersonic weapon. The test follows a similar launch by North Korea and comes after three South Korean soldiers were injured by land mine explosions near the border. North Korea denies involvement in the incident. The hypersonic test marks a major step in South Korea's defense capabilities. As both Koreas advance their military technology, the risk of escalation grows. Regional stability now hinges on how these developments are managed.

South Korea is recalling its ambassador to Ukraine in response to President Zelenskyy's disclosure of a secret transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war to Seoul. The move underscores Seoul's concern over the handling of sensitive intelligence. Diplomatic trust between allies may be affected by such public revelations. The dispute highlights the complexity of managing international cooperation on security matters. How Ukraine responds could shape future intelligence sharing.

A new era in Hollywood begins as Paramount and Warner Bros. complete their mega merger under the new entity Skydance. The deal has cleared court challenges, regulatory reviews, and public opposition. This consolidation creates one of the most powerful entertainment companies in history. The merger could reshape how movies and shows are made and distributed. Industry watchers will monitor its impact on creativity and competition.

Google has introduced a new AI tool called the Gemini agent, designed to help workers with everyday tasks. It can write and run code, and connect with apps like Gmail to streamline workflows. The agent aims to boost productivity by automating routine digital chores. As AI becomes more embedded in office life, questions arise about job roles and data security. Users will soon see how well it integrates into daily work.

Former national intelligence chief Jay Clayton has been named the new AI czar by President Trump. He will lead the White House's 'Super Intelligence Force', a new group coordinating the government's work with artificial intelligence. The role reflects growing focus on AI in national strategy. How this office shapes policy could affect technology development across sectors. The appointment signals a push to centralize AI oversight.

Four astronauts have returned to Earth after spending eight months aboard the International Space Station. Their mission included scientific experiments and maintenance work in orbit. The safe return marks another successful long-duration spaceflight. These missions help prepare for future deep-space exploration. The crew will now undergo medical checks and debriefings.

The Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen, a Belgian-born scientist at the University of Wisconsin, Madison. He is honored for his work on neutrinos, elusive cosmic particles that help explain the universe's origins. His research has advanced understanding of high-energy processes in space. The findings open new windows into how the cosmos evolved. Halzen's work continues to influence astrophysics.
