# World, Business and 4 more

This is Singularity. Today, we cover the Nobel Peace Prize going to a South African human rights lawyer, a massive wine theft in Italy, Novak Djokovic’s early exit in Shanghai, and the recovery of two stolen Renoir paintings. We’ll also look at reactions to an upcoming execution, attacks on a Saudi airport, AI monitoring in Gaza classrooms, safety disputes at OpenAI, the NFL’s stance on prediction markets, and inflation under Trump. Then, the legacy of a pioneering software engineer, data center plans in Australia, astronauts returning from space, Hurricane Isaias approaching the Gulf Coast, new materials for energy tech, a bacterial defense against viruses, the death of an Indian film star, a Canadian writer’s Nobel win, a rugby coach’s new role, and the passing of an NFL legend.

South African human rights lawyer Navi Pillay has won the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize. She is recognized for her work advancing human rights and international law over decades. Her career includes key roles at the United Nations and in global tribunals. This award highlights the impact of legal advocacy in protecting people worldwide. The prize underscores the ongoing importance of holding power to account through justice.

Thieves have stolen about 30,000 bottles of premium wine from a Marchesi Antinori winery in Tuscany. The haul is worth an estimated 5 million euros, making it the largest wine theft in Italy’s history. The company says the stolen vintages are high-end and traceable. Such a large-scale heist raises concerns about security in the luxury goods sector. Authorities are investigating how the theft was carried out and who may be behind it.

Novak Djokovic has suffered a surprise loss in the second round of the Shanghai Masters. He was defeated by Hubert Hurkacz just three days after winning the China Open in Beijing. The early exit interrupts what had been a strong run for the top-ranked player. It also opens the tournament to new contenders. Fans will watch how Djokovic responds in his next appearances on the tour.

Two Renoir paintings stolen from a museum in southern France last month have been recovered. Local authorities say six people have been arrested in connection with the theft. The artworks were taken during a break-in and had been missing for weeks. Their return is a relief for the cultural community and law enforcement. The case shows how art theft investigations can take time but sometimes end in success.

Alonzo Lunsford, a survivor of the Fort Hood shooting, supports the gunman’s execution by firing squad. He told the BBC the punishment fits the crime. However, he has concerns about whether the execution should be livestreamed. He worries about the effect on victims’ families if it becomes public viewing. The debate touches on justice, privacy, and the role of media in capital punishment.

Saudi authorities report that three people were killed in attacks on Riyadh airport. The Houthi rebels have claimed responsibility for the two attacks on Thursday. Several others were wounded in the incidents. The General Authority of Civil Aviation confirmed the casualties. The strikes raise regional security concerns and could affect air travel in the area.

A U.S. charity operating in Gaza plans to use artificial intelligence to monitor speech in its classrooms. The system is meant to detect any signs of hateful content from teachers. The charity says its curriculum promotes peace and tolerance. The move has sparked debate over surveillance and trust in education. Critics question whether AI can fairly judge speech in complex cultural settings.

OpenAI has fired three safety researchers following a dispute over AI risks. The company cited a breach of trust as the reason for the dismissals. The researchers had accused OpenAI of putting corporate interests ahead of safety. Their departure has raised questions about how AI firms balance innovation and oversight. The situation may influence how other tech companies handle internal disagreements.

The NFL has filed a legal brief urging the Supreme Court to rule on the regulation of prediction markets. It argues that states are better suited to oversee sports-related contracts. The league is concerned about how these markets could affect the integrity of games. The case could shape how betting and data are managed in professional sports. A decision may set a precedent for other industries as well.

Just weeks before the midterm elections, 90% of Americans say prices are the main factor shaping their view of the economy. This reflects widespread concern about inflation and cost of living. The data shows how economic conditions influence voter sentiment. It also highlights the challenge of meeting campaign promises on affordability. The trend suggests economic issues will play a major role at the polls.

Margaret Hamilton, the computer scientist whose software helped power the Apollo 11 moon landing, has died at the age of 90. She led the team that developed onboard flight software for NASA. Her work was critical to the success of the first lunar mission. She later received the Presidential Medal of Freedom for her contributions. Her legacy lives on in modern computing and space exploration.

American tech companies face legal and public pushback at home as they seek to build data centers in Australia. These facilities store and process large amounts of digital information. While the firms see opportunity abroad, Australians are questioning the environmental and regulatory impact. Local opposition could slow or reshape these projects. The outcome may set a pattern for how global tech expansion is received.

Four astronauts have returned to Earth after spending eight months aboard the International Space Station. Their mission included scientific experiments and maintenance work. The safe landing marks another successful long-duration spaceflight. Such missions help prepare for future exploration beyond low Earth orbit. The return also allows scientists to study the effects of extended time in space.

Hurricane Isaias is now a Category 2 storm and is moving toward the Gulf Coast. It is the first hurricane in the Atlantic basin this year. Millions in its path are preparing for heavy rain, strong winds, and possible flooding. Forecasters are urging residents to follow evacuation orders. The storm’s strength and track will be closely monitored in the coming hours.

Scientists have discovered new materials that form briefly during heating processes. These “in-between” structures were previously undetected by standard methods. One find is a previously unknown form of bismuth, which could improve battery and solar fuel technology. The discovery opens new paths for energy storage and conversion. The research may lead to more efficient and sustainable materials.

Researchers have found that bacteria can use a virus’s own weapon against it. A virus-produced enzyme cuts a bacterial protein, which triggers a self-destruct response in the infected cell. This stops the virus from spreading to other bacteria. The mechanism reveals a new aspect of microbial defense systems. It could help improve phage therapies used to treat bacterial infections.

Indian actor Nana Patekar has died suddenly at the age of 75. He was known for his powerful and intense performances in Hindi and Marathi cinema. Over his long career, he became a distinctive presence in Indian film. His roles often explored social issues and complex characters. Tributes are pouring in from across the entertainment industry.

Canadian writer Anne Carson has won the Nobel Prize in literature. The 76-year-old from Toronto blends poetry, essay, and fiction in her work. She often draws on ancient Greek and Roman themes and stories. Her writing is known for its originality and depth. The award recognizes her unique voice in contemporary literature.

Shaun Edwards has joined Argentina’s rugby team as defence coach. His contract runs until the end of the 2027 Rugby World Cup in Australia. Edwards brings experience from top-level European rugby. His role will focus on strengthening the team’s defensive strategies. The appointment signals Argentina’s ambitions on the international stage.

Mike Ditka, the Super Bowl-winning player and coach, has died at the age of 86. He led the Chicago Bears to victory in Super Bowl XX in 1986. Ditka was a dominant figure in the NFL both on and off the field. His legacy includes shaping one of the most iconic teams in football history. Fans and players are remembering his impact on the sport.

That’s all for this episode of Singularity.
