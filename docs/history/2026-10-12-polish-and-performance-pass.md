# 2026-10-11 and 12: Polish and performance pass

The owner said the boxes looked like "ai slop", asked for the player's transcript and chapters to get a top margin and a bottom fade, for the cover and the controls to sit closer, for Ada to go as a host name (a male voice made it wrong), for names that follow the chosen voice, for a second voice on the discussion, for a Settings page that is not ugly and chips that can be selected, for the "Live curated" chip and the wait for suggested sources to go, and then for every page, screen and component to be polished until it looks like a real Google app. A later message asked for a deep look at performance: podcast generation, time to play, suggested sources, and the everyday delays. Late in the session the owner added: "beautiful empty state illustration with title and an action button", and then, on seeing the first scenes, "keep the illustrations simple". Last of all the owner called the test suite "ridiculous for a simple application", asked for tests that are "lean, impactful and meaningful", and then for a postmortem so that the next agents do not repeat the mistake. Decisions: D72 to D100.

## How it was done

- One design layer first (D72, D73): tonal containers, state layers, pill buttons, a type scale. Everything else was built on it, so the pages could be polished in parallel without drifting apart.
- Workers in isolated git worktrees, one area each (Library, Today and episodes, Create, the player, Settings and Topics, the shell and its empty states, suggested sources, host names, three performance sets). Their commits were cherry-picked into the session branch, each time followed by typecheck, lint, the unit suites and the specs the change touched.
- Nine critic rounds (read-only reviewers, on screenshots of every screen at phone and desktop width in both themes, then driven live on the fixture build) found about 260 problems, ranked P1 to P3; each round was fixed by workers (or by the lead, for small ones) before the next, and a later round first checked that the earlier fixes held. An automated audit (axe, tap targets, overflow) ran beside them. P1s per round: round four three (a lost second edit inside the server's gap, a page that scrolled away from a tap, a rundown counted as a story), round five five (a form pushed off a phone by a feed address, an episode's actions live offline, the Library's search matching letters inside words, an Up next that played nothing, and an open page that scrolled itself at every turn), round six four (after any run every Library control was dead; a refused save read Added after a reload; an offline settings change vanished; and a missing audio file showed and spoke the browser's "MEDIA_ELEMENT_ERROR", which the previous round's own fix had brought in), round seven two (an item stored as Played before a word of it was heard, from the unlock primer's silence; and a settings page first opened offline in a later session with no editor, because the service worker had never saved a file fetched at run time). Round eight found one more (a held change refused after its page was left kept reading Saving) and round nine one (a refused model choice read Saved after a reload). Round six was the first to walk whole journeys across screens rather than one screen, and found what the screen-by-screen rounds could not: the joins. Not one of these P1s was caught by the test suite (the postmortem has the table).

## What the critics found, and why it mattered

- Outlines on every control, and hover and selected fills at about 1.03:1 from the page, so a chip could not be told from a selected one. Fix: tonal containers and an accent container for the chosen or current state, pinned by a contrast test (D72).
- Type: headline and summary within a pixel of each other on a phone, three sizes between 15 and 17px, weight 500 on everything. Fix: a scale with visible steps (D73).
- Reading pages had two Play buttons on a phone, a rule above every group and a hero that changed layout with the length of the title (D77).
- A scroll fade that never ran in Chrome (the minifier folded the timeline into the animation shorthand) and a keyboard focus ring that vanished under an opacity-based hover layer: both fixed, the first with a test (learnings, 2026-10-11).
- Settings read as a form, not a list; a source's score was drawn three ways at once; a new topic opened a page a static site could not yet have (D79).
- Legacy discussions still named Ada and Leo although the voices said otherwise; new ones take names from the voices (D74).
- Round four and five, by area. Settings: a second edit made inside the server's ten second gap was lost on Back while the page said Saved, and Format on Daily was a choice the product ignored (D91); a feed address widened the Add source form past a phone; a model's name was cut to a different model's. Player: Next scrolled a phone page by 601px, so a second tap seeked backwards; the rundown counted as story 1; two Pause discs under the open page; a Try again button that drew a play triangle (D92). Reading: a tab's jump landed 56px low because the bar hid after the heading was placed; hyphenated words made headlines lopsided; offline switched off one run starter and left three; stories stepped over with Play from here were marked played (D93). Library and Create: "ai" found every card; a match line added 20 to 40px above a list title; a segmented control grew as its check came in.

- Round six and seven, by area. Joins between screens: the Library's root hook was also on the run line's link, so after a run its search, filters and view did nothing; Today's chip, the floating Play and the Library's card each said something else about the same recording; the stories heard lost their Played marks on a reload; a finished rewrite said "Live in about a minute" for the life of the tab, also after the episode was deleted (D93, D97). Errors: a standing refusal offered Try again on the hero chip while the disc said Play; a passing 503 was treated as permanent until a reload; the room the previous round kept for the notice made the open page scroll on common laptop windows; "You are offline" stayed after the connection came back; Next out of a failure announced the next story and played nothing (D94, D97).
- The lab-only player specs (`astro dev`, not the static build) had not been run in the session, and five of them asserted the old played marks; the first full run of them found those, a missing history bridge on the lab page, and a real Tab order bug on the phone bar.

## Empty states

The owner's request came late, when most screens were done. The first set was a card and a magnifier with a ground shadow, floating marks, a shelf, a waveform and a clock badge; it looked like a template. After "keep the illustrations simple" each scene became one flat object of two to four shapes in the theme's own tones (D96), at a third to half the bytes, with the pattern kept: art, title, one line, one filled action. The Today and Library scenes can only be seen in a copy of the site with its fixtures emptied; the build the other pages use always has content.

## The test suite

The owner's complaint came after the last critic round. The lead had four read-only auditors (Opus, about 30 minutes each, one per layer: engine and packages, web unit, site e2e, player e2e and its lab) read the whole suite against eight categories: change detectors, tests of the platform, duplicates, table-driven candidates, brittle tests, over-specified mocks, dead or dev-only targets and trivia. They also named what to protect. The unit layer was mostly earned (88% of the engine's tests and 92% of the web unit tests kept); the browser layers were not (29% of the site e2e declarations and 41% of the player e2e ones to delete, a quarter more to merge). The owner then said "skip anything possible, keep tests lean impactful and meaningful", and four workers cut one layer each in their own worktrees, hitting their turn limit once or twice and being resumed with the instruction to commit first. The lead merged them (no conflicts; one broken import, `./lab`, found by running the new specs) and checked the result.

- Results (D99): browser runs from 1,935 listed to 429 and their wall time from about 32 minutes to about 10; unit tests from 4,813 to 4,601 (95 s to 64 s); the lab, 23 unused exports and options, the Settings sheet and its install hint deleted; the 10.25 s edit gap, scrypt and the live config out of the tests.
- Worker decisions worth knowing: the passphrase verifier stays real with a cheap hash instead of a fake; the shared handler gate was not extracted because the four gates differ; `createWorkflowDispatcher` now goes through the shared GitHub client.
- Re-break check: for each fix of this branch that the audit's cuts could have orphaned, the lead reverted only its source in a scratch worktree and ran the surviving tests. A surviving test failed each time (the postmortem names them); the service worker's warm depth on its own and the unlock primer in a browser were not guarded before the cuts either.
- Why it happened and how not to repeat it: [the postmortem](../engineering/postmortem-test-suite.md). The short form is in `AGENTS.md`.

## Performance: where the time went

The analyst measured from workflow history and the fixture build (production was unreachable from the sandbox). A custom episode took about 2.2 minutes at the median from the press to the page: runner setup 14 to 17 s, the podcast step 58 to 87 s (almost all the writer), the Vercel build 31 to 34 s, the page noticing the deploy about 6 s later. Play took 0.5 to 1 s with stored audio and 3 to 6 s without (every recent briefing, because `audio.yml` fails at `vercel link` on a dead token), and a tap in the first two seconds of a page was lost. A setting looked unsaved for about a minute.

- The writer rewrote almost every episode for nothing: it was asked for 90% of what the stories can carry, models delivered 79 to 87%, and the floor was 80% (D80).
- The workflows installed all 445 packages to run one CLI (D81).
- The page noticed a deploy within 10 s, the service worker cached `/latest.json` under every query string, a new episode was never pre-narrated, and an early Play tap was dropped (D82).
- Saved settings showed old values for a minute, and a new topic's suggestions waited for a rebuild (D79).

Not done, in rough order of value: one fetch for both briefing passes, a thin-story rewrite instead of a whole one, a long first turn split at its first sentence, the sheet and player code loaded on intent, a shallow checkout, and the Vercel install. They are listed in `state.md`, Next.

## Time zone, Format and the second voice

Late on 2026-10-12 the owner asked for the time zone selector back ("I don't know if you have an argument against it"), for the second voice on Daily and Weekly, and then for the second voice's play button. The argument against was D89's: a selector the engine ignores is a control that does nothing. So the zone came back only once the engine read it (D100): the gate, the digest ids, the weekly slot, the cron route, the Next episode line and the summaries count in the schedule's zone, while the narration caps, the usage month, pruning and scoring stay on Stockholm. Two workers ran in parallel worktrees on disjoint sections of the same settings files and merged with one import conflict. Format and the Discussion voice came back as rows under Episode; they are saved, but a briefing is still read from its digest by one voice, so they act only in a discussion (D91's reason still holds; the owner chose them anyway, so D100 and Next record the gap). The play button was already in the code but hidden for a voice with no sample, which made the second voice look as if it had none; it is now always on the row and dimmed when there is no sample.

## Not verified

Nothing here ran against live services, a real phone or Safari. The time zone, Format and Discussion voice work was merged when the owner asked to skip tests and see it on the domain: the unit suite, typecheck, Biome, the real build and the Settings browser specs (122 runs) ran on it; the full site and player browser suites did not, and nothing ran on the Vercel cron in a non-Stockholm zone. The timing savings are reasoned from the code or measured on the fixture build. The writer change (D80) has only run with fakes; the first real episode after it is the test.
