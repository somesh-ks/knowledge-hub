# 2026-11-03: the avatar answers a tap

The owner asked that a tapped avatar make faces, morph into other shapes and come back, or do something small like waving or looking through binoculars, by mood and hue, kept lean but with variety (D353).

What was built. `avatar.ts` draws hidden parts in every avatar (a hand, binoculars on the shape's own eyes, brows). `avatar.css` plays the shows on `data-play`: wink, wow, wave, scout, boo and morph; each mood lists its own in `--av-plays`. `avatarReactions.ts` holds the two pure choices (which show, which shapes a morph visits), `accountAvatar.ts` plays them: one tap plays the next show of the list from a start the hue's band sets, a morph swaps shapes and ends on the reader's own, and a tap in the app bar plays on the profile after arrival. Reduced motion and offline play nothing.

Choices. Ordered, not random: a repeat reads as a bug and a fixed order is testable. No new duration token (llms.txt was 7 bytes from its 14 KB cap); the shows reuse the look, hop, spark and spring-pop durations. The show rules sit after the state rules so they win over the bob while audio plays. The props are the body colour (the hand) and the face colour (the binoculars), so they follow every mood and hue by the tokens.

Checks. One unit test for the two choices, one browser journey (five taps give different shows, a morph visits other shapes and ends home, nothing plays for a calm reader). Clips per mood from the built site were sent for approval before any PR.

Second pass, same day. The owner saw the first clips and said they looked bad. Looking frame by frame: the hand was a thin stroke and a dot that faded in and rotated stiffly, the binoculars were two flat rings that read as glasses and popped in, the spin was a plain turn, and a morph swapped shapes at full size and then popped. Every show now winds up, acts and settles with one overshoot, and parts lag the body (the mitten trails the arm, the eyes pop after the jump). The hand is a mitten on a thick arm, the binoculars are dark barrels with glinting lenses raised from below, brows came in for wow, wink and boo, and a morph squashes flat, swaps under a breath of blur and stretches out. A second tap queues one show instead of being dropped. Frames were checked on a paused fake clock (Playwright clock plus paused Web Animations), on the cookie, star, heart, burst, ghost, bat, cat, skull, cauldron and pumpkin. The minifier writes 1500ms as 1.5s, so lengths read from tokens parse both units.

Third pass. The owner found the second pass too springy and asked for calm, on brand motion (docs/specs/motion.md). The wind-ups, squash and stretch, overshoots and the boo shudder went; moves now take `--ease-in-out` or the 1.5% spatial springs and fades the effects springs. Spin and hop left the tap lists. The morph is a quiet scale and opacity ease with the swap at its lowest point, through one other shape instead of two. The bat's wave arm is smaller and sits on its wing.

Fourth pass. The owner rejected the third: the morph was a cut and the faces did not show the app's quality. Critique: the morph scaled one shape away and another in, so no outline ever changed; the expressions were rigid transforms of a whole face (a tilt, a scale) with nothing in the eyes or mouth; the props read as stickers; everything moved at once, so nothing led; the shows were too alike to tell apart. Now the outline itself flows (rings of 96 points aligned clockwise from the top, the face gliding with it, colours mixed in oklab, the swap hidden under a blink), the props are gone, and the face acts: eyes that glance, widen and blink, cheeks that rise into a squint, and one mouth path that changes between smile, grin and o. The eyes lead the body by a stagger and the body leads the shape. `avatarMotion.ts` holds the pure parts with a unit test (the spring stops, the beats, the mouth at rest equal to the drawn smile, rings that pair whichever way they were drawn). Frames were checked at 30 fps on cookie, star, heart, flower, ghost, cat, pumpkin, skull, bat and cauldron. The class guard bars a script from writing the Avatar's classes, so `avatarParts()` in `design-system/avatar.ts` finds the parts a show moves.

Headphones (D358). The owner asked that the avatar wear headphones while a podcast plays. A band behind the head and a cup on each side, placed per shape and checked on all 15 in three moods, light and dark: the face's ink vanished on a dark page, so they take the page's ink. They fade and settle in on the springs, ride the bob, and a morph veils them like a stem.
